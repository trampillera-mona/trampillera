The intent is controlled by clear attribution and brand separation, not by restricting the number of downloads. The MIT License permits broad use, modification, self-hosting, and redistribution, including commercial use, subject to its terms.

## 2. What the MIT License does not grant

The software license does not grant trademark rights or permission to present an independent project as an official TRAMPILLERA product. Brand identifiers are addressed separately in [TRADEMARKS.md](./TRADEMARKS.md).

The license also does not override license terms that apply to third-party dependencies, fonts, media, datasets, or other externally sourced materials.

## 3. Attribution for redistributed versions

Redistributors must preserve the applicable copyright and license notices as required by the MIT License. Modified distributions should make their changes clear and must not falsely imply official endorsement, certification, operation, or support by TRAMPILLERA.

## 4. No separate proprietary Core at this stage

The current repository is one deployable application. No directory or component is designated here as a closed-source proprietary Core. A future proprietary component must be explicitly identified, separated from MIT-licensed files, and distributed under separately published terms. A later license change does not retroactively cancel permissions already granted for earlier MIT-licensed releases.

## 5. Scope and legal review

This document explains the project's intended distribution model; it is not a substitute for legal advice. Before commercial launch, the project owner should confirm the correct legal copyright holder, ownership or authorization for brand assets, and the license status of all bundled third-party materials.
