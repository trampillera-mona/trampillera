## 1. World premise

An expedition has established a small foothold on the edge of Arcadia Planitia. Habitat 01 is intact. The supply route crosses unfamiliar terrain, and three provisional points of interest have been marked for investigation. The expedition must balance safety, science, construction and logistics while its understanding of the surrounding territory evolves.

This dossier expands the existing open scenario **The Quiet Landing**. It does not establish a canonical ending, mandatory rules or a single correct way to play.

## 4. The six map layers

1. **Terrain** — contours, crater field and ridge.
2. **Infrastructure** — habitat, landing structures and route.
3. **Resources** — provisional survey points, not confirmed deposits.
4. **Operations** — rover position, routes and objectives.
5. **Environment** — illustrative dust and visibility hazard.
6. **Scenario / Time** — event markers and changing state.

The six overlays share a canvas, map frame, coordinate grid and north orientation. Use tracing paper or transparent film for simultaneous physical overlays. Print at actual size; do not stretch or crop individual sheets.

## 5. Expedition roles

Choose roles to suit the group. Roles are prompts, not character classes or locked abilities.

- **Safety lead:** tracks crew exposure, route risk and shelter decisions.
- **Survey lead:** records observations and distinguishes confirmed findings from hypotheses.
- **Construction lead:** plans habitat extensions, landing-area improvements and repairs.
- **Logistics lead:** tracks supplies, travel priorities and return plans.
- **Recorder:** maintains the expedition log and marks changes on the appropriate layer.

One player may hold several roles. Players may invent alternatives.

## 6. Open objectives

Choose any combination:

- Survey two provisional points of interest.
- Establish a safe route between Habitat 01 and one point of interest.
- Identify and mark a hazard zone on the environment layer.
- Return the rover to the habitat with a written expedition log.
- Propose one infrastructure improvement and record its trade-offs.
- Revisit an earlier assumption and update its confidence.

## 7. Event prompts

At the end of a turn, choose or roll one prompt and agree how it affects the scenario:

- Dust reduces visibility; add a temporary hazard overlay.
- Communications delay requires the next decision to be written before the result is revealed.
- A damaged wheel forces a route change or repair decision.
- A new signal creates a survey opportunity but does not reveal its source.
- A supply discrepancy requires the logistics lead to reconcile the inventory.
- A clear observation window allows the crew to revise one provisional map note.

These are narrative prompts, not a tested probability or resource economy.

## 8. State and evidence discipline

Use these labels in the expedition log:

- **KNOWN** — established by the agreed starting scenario or directly observed during play.
- **PROVISIONAL** — plausible but not yet confirmed.
- **UNKNOWN** — no reliable information yet.
- **CHANGED** — a previously recorded state has been updated; preserve the earlier entry.
- **DISPUTED** — players have different interpretations; retain both until resolved.

Do not silently convert a provisional resource marker into a confirmed deposit. Record who made an observation, when it was made in the session, which coordinate it concerns and what evidence supports it.

## 9. Physical play loop

1. Set out the base map and selected overlays.
2. Place Habitat 01, the rover and provisional point-of-interest markers.
3. Agree roles and objectives.
4. Make one decision at a time; write down its expected consequence.
5. Resolve the consequence using rules agreed by the group.
6. Update the relevant layer and expedition log.
7. Preserve uncertain information as uncertain.
8. End with a short debrief: what changed, what remains unknown and what should happen next?
